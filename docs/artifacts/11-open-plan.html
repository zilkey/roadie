Business plan · draft · Oct 2026

We don't want your money.

The Ingrid Avison Band is building a booking, touring and band-money app for ourselves. We're giving it to every working musician, and giving the code, the design and the ideas to anyone who wants to copy them.

What it is

One place for the business side of a band: venues, festivals and the people who book them; a Pitch List of what we're going after; emails sent from your own Gmail; tours with drives, flights and places to stay; shows with a run of show, gear and setlists; songs and rehearsal recordings; and every dollar in and out, with payouts to the people who played.

We're building it because we need it. Jeff and Ingrid book, route and pay the band out of spreadsheets and pages that don't talk to each other. If it works for a five-piece out of Boulder, it will work for a lot of bands.

Built by a touring bandEvery feature exists because we needed it on a real tour first.
Your data stays yoursExport everything any time. No ads, no selling data, no investors.
AI does the clickingResearch venues, draft pitches and fill in drive times with any AI model you like.

Open, with zero restrictions

Copying us is the point. Other companies, other bands and other developers can take the code, the screens, the data model and the ideas, change them, sell them, and never tell us.

  • Code: public on GitHub under a public-domain-style license (0BSD or the Unlicense), so there's no attribution clause to follow.
  • Designs and docs: CC0. The blueprint, the wireframes and this plan included.
  • Concepts: how pitches become shows, how tours hold options, how the band ledger never edits a line. No patents, ever.
  • Self-hosting: one Firebase project per band, so anyone can run their own copy and pay their own bill.

The one thing we may hold onto is the name, so people can tell which copy is ours. That's an open question below.

How money works

We don't make money. We try not to lose it.

The app costs almost nothing to run at our size. As more artists use it, the cloud bill grows, and we cover it in this order:

1We pay itWhile the bill is small, the band covers it.
2GrantsArts councils, open source funds and music organizations. A grant that covers hosting means nobody pays.
3Cost shareIf grants don't cover it, we take a recent cloud bill, divide it by the number of active artists, and charge that, in multi-month blocks so card fees don't eat it.

Cost share is not a price. It's the bill, divided, published. When the bill drops, the share drops. When a grant lands, the share pauses.

Example numbers, not our real bill. A card processor takes roughly 3% plus 30¢ per charge, which is why we bill a few months at once.

If it blows up

This plan is for a project run by two musicians in their spare time. If thousands of bands show up and it needs people to keep it running, the plan changes, in the open.

  • Nonprofit first. We'd look at becoming a nonprofit, likely starting under a fiscal sponsor so grants and donations can come in before we set one up ourselves.
  • Staff costs join the bill. If we hire, salaries go on the published cost page and into the cost share, with the same math.
  • Never a lock-in. The code stays open and self-hosting stays possible, whatever the money looks like.
  • We'll say so before it happens. Any change to how money works is announced, with numbers, before it takes effect.

Not legal or tax advice; we'll talk to people who know before choosing a structure.

Marketing plan

Our pitch is the business model. Musicians are tired of tools that take a cut, raise prices, or get bought. We lead with the opposite.

"We don't want your money. Here's our cloud bill."

The open cost page

A public page with the latest cloud bill, the number of active artists, the cost share, and what grants covered. Updated every month, kept forever.

Tour videos

Short videos of us on the road using the app: booking the open date, the day sheet in the van, settling up after the show. The Mountain West run in March 2027 is the first one.

Webinars

Live sessions that walk through it: set up your band, build your Pitch List, plan a tour, pay your players. Recorded and posted.

Musician to musician

Bands we share bills with, players who sub for us, the bookers we work with. Every show is a demo.

Open source crowd

A clean repo, a public roadmap, good first issues. Developers who play music are our best early users.

Build in public

Posts about what we built, what it cost, and what broke. The cost page and the changelog are the content.

Later: a place to talk

Eventually we'll build spaces where working musicians and indie artists can talk about the job: routing, pay, which rooms treat bands well, how to split money fairly, how to keep going. Not before the app is solid, and run with the same openness.

How we'll know it's working

  • Active artists, and how many play a show booked through the app each month.
  • Cost per artist, and how much of the bill grants cover.
  • Forks and copies. Other people taking the ideas counts as a win.
  • Webinar attendance and who sets up a band afterward.

Later

Community

Spaces for working musicians to talk. Nonprofit if it needs staff.

If

we merge with Tenor

Tenor is Daphne's project. If we join forces, we'll also build a huge open source venue list that every artist can use, rate and add to.

  • Open venue data. Rooms, capacities, sound, load-in, ages, who books them. Free to download and reuse, like the code.
  • Ratings from artists. Pay, sound, hospitality, how the night went. The ratings our app already asks for after each show, shared if the band chooses.
  • Shared info. Parking, load-in tips, what the booker likes in a pitch. Private booker contacts stay private unless they agree to be listed.
  • Plugs into the app. A band's venue book starts from the shared list, and their own notes sit on top.

This is a big if. Nothing here depends on it.

Open questions

Do we keep the name as the one restriction, or let anyone use it too?
Hosted service first, or self-hosting first with a hosted option later?
Which grants fit a free tool for musicians, and who writes the applications?
How long a block for cost share: 6 months, 12, or the artist's choice?
What do artists who can't pay the share get? Our default answer: the same app.
Does the project stay under our band, or get its own entity from the start?
