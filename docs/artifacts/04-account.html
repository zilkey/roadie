    How it works

    One app, many bands. Jeff plays keys in the Ingrid Avison Band and subs in The Low Tides. Ingrid runs the band and could start a solo act. David plays bass for Ingrid and runs The Low Tides. A booking agent books two of them. Each person has a different job and a different level of access in each band, and plenty of them never log in.

    The model

    • An artist is a band or a solo act. It's the wall around everything: its shows, pitches, venues, songs, money and settings all live under artists/{artistId}. Nothing leaks between bands.
    • A member is a person's place in one band. Name, what they do there (roles: musician, singer, manager, booking agent, tour manager, engineer, merch), what they play, and their access. The same human in two bands is two member records.
    • A user is a login. Members who sign in point to the same users/{uid}, which is how "my shows across all my bands" works. Subs and players who never log in are members with no user.
    • Access is per band. For now each member is admin, member, or no login. Ingrid is admin of her band and could be admin of her solo act; David is admin of The Low Tides and a member of Ingrid's band.
    • Roles are what you do; access is what you can touch. A booking agent is a role. Today an agent is given admin or member access; with RBAC later, an "agent" permission set can see booking and shows without the money.
    • Agencies and managers can be organizations later. orgs/{orgId} groups the people at an agency or management company, and a member record can say which org they're from. Their access still comes from the member record in each band.
    • Rules read the sign-in token. The server keeps a claim like artists: {iab: "admin", lowtides: "member"} on each login. If someone is in more bands than fit in a token, rules fall back to artists/{artistId}/access/{uid}, a mirror the server keeps.
    • RBAC is a swap, not a rewrite. Every server command already asks can(user, artistId, "money.write"). Today admin and member are two fixed lists of permissions; later bands define their own roles from the same list.
    • Busy, not why. When an admin asks who's free, a musician who's playing with another band that night shows as "busy elsewhere", with no band or venue named.
    • Booking data stays per band for now. Venues and contacts belong to each artist; "copy to my other band" moves them across. A shared venue list is the Tenor "if" in the business plan.
