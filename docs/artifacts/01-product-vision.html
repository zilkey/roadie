Band App: vision and principles

Laid out the way Marty Cagan separates them: a vision that says where we're going in 3 to 10 years, principles that say what we stand for and break ties when good goals pull in different directions, and a strategy for which steps come first. The principles are ranked: when two collide, the higher one wins.

Vision
Every band and solo artist has insanely good tools to run their career and their day.

Booking, touring, shows, songs, money, and eventually getting the word out, at the level a label's back office would give a big act. Free, open, and as easy to talk to as a bandmate.

The problem

Working musicians run a small business out of spreadsheets, text threads, email and memory. Booking lives in one place, routing in another, the money in a notes app, and the setlist on a phone that dies at soundcheck. The tools that exist either take a cut, cost a monthly fee bands resent, or are built for agencies, not for the people on stage.

The work isn't hard. It's scattered, it happens at bad times (in the van, at the merch table at midnight), and the people doing it would rather be playing.

Who it's for first

Independent bands and solo artists who book themselves, from the weekly local gig to the six-state van tour.

Who comes with them

The players and subs who need the time, address and setlist; the manager and the booking agent who work for them.

Who it's built by

The Ingrid Avison Band, using it on our own shows and tours before anyone else does.

Principles

Ranked. Each one says what we believe, what it costs us, and what it means in the product.

1

We serve musicians. We don't make money from them.

The product exists for the people using it. Money only comes in to keep the lights on, and we show exactly where it goes.

Even over growth and revenue. No ads, no selling data, no investors, no lock-in. If a feature would earn money by costing musicians money, attention or ownership of their data, we don't build it.

  • Open source with no restrictions; anyone can copy the code, the screens and the ideas.
  • Hosting is covered by grants first, then a published cost share. The cloud bill is public.
  • Export everything, any time. Self-hosting stays possible.
2

We never waste a musician's time.

Every tap, form and question has to earn its place. Musicians use this tired, on bad signal, between other jobs, and they fat-finger things.

Even over completeness and power. When a feature makes the simple case slower, the simple case wins and the power moves behind More. A short form beats a thorough one.

  • Nothing needs a Save button. Fields autosave, new forms are drafts that survive closing the app and switching devices.
  • Every change can be undone, including everything an AI session did.
  • Setup asks the big questions first and only the follow-ups that matter; unused features stay hidden.
  • The post-show interview asks only what's missing, one question at a time.
3

AI first. Never AI only.

The best interface for a musician in a van is a sentence. Talk to your favorite chatbot, Claude, Gemini or another, or click around the app. Both do the same things.

Even over a polished UI for every edge case. Every action is a server command an AI can call before it's a screen. But nothing requires an AI, and sending anything on someone's behalf stays with a person.

  • One MCP server any assistant can use, with the same commands the app calls.
  • Every write is signed with the person and the assistant, so you can see and undo what an AI did.
  • Claude drafts emails and asks; you send them.
Why this order: serving musicians decides what we build at all. Not wasting their time decides how. AI first is our best way to save that time, so it gives way when it wouldn't.

Proposed principles

Two more that the decisions so far already follow. Keep, merge, or drop them; Cagan's advice is to keep the list short enough to remember.

4

Never lose or corrupt their data.

A wrong number in the band's money or a lost show is worse than a missing feature.

Even over speed of building. Strict shapes, server-only writes, a ledger that's never edited, history on every record, nightly backups.

5

Built on the road.

We use it on our own shows and tours first. If it doesn't work in the van, it doesn't ship.

Even over what other bands ask for, until we've lived it. Requests are welcome; we test them on a real night first.

Strategy

The order we'll go in. Each step earns the next.

Now

Run our band on it

Booking, pitches, shows, schedules, tours, money, songs. Talking to Claude as the main way in.

Next

Bands we play with

Many bands in one app, each walled off. Setup that starts simple. The open cost page.

Then

Any band, anywhere

Public launch with tour videos and webinars. Grants. Docs that build themselves.

Eventually

Getting the word out

Replace Buffer: schedule posts for shows, tours and releases to every social account from the same app, written with Claude from the show details already there.

If it happens

An open venue list

With Tenor: venues every artist can rate and add to. And places to talk about the job.

How we document

Docs build themselves from end-to-end tests, so they never fall behind the app.

Playwright walks the stories

One script per user story runs against the Firebase emulator with seeded example data, the Mountain West tour, and takes a screenshot at every step, on phone and desktop, light and dark.

Claude Code writes the words

Each step gets a doc comment saying what the musician sees and why, written by Claude Code from the code and the screens. Step names become headings.

Rebuilt on every merge

CI runs the scripts, which double as tests, and publishes the docs site. A screen change that breaks a step breaks the build, so stale docs can't ship.

Talk examples too

The same scripts record what to say to Claude for each story, so every doc page shows both ways to do it.

Not doing

  • Ticketing or taking a cut of anything a band earns.
  • Ads, sponsored placements, or selling anyone's data.
  • Features for agencies that make the band's own use harder.
  • Anything that only works with one AI company's assistant.
