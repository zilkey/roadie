Band App · spec · Oct 2026

Roadie

Roadie is the band app's built-in assistant: a full-height panel on the left of every desktop screen, always there for admins. It does what Claude does through the MCP server, but inside the app: it can take you to a page, fill in a form for you to check, and change records you watch update as it goes. It talks straight to the same command layer as every button.

The window

  • A panel on the left, always there for admins. On desktop Roadie is a full-height column about 300px wide, to the left of the menu, on every screen. It can be narrowed to a slim strip but not closed. Members never see it; the server refuses them too.
  • It stays put. The panel lives in the app's root layout, so moving to another page doesn't touch it or the conversation. After a reload it comes back the same width, on the same conversation.
  • It knows where you are. Every message carries the page you're on, the record you're looking at, and any form that's open with what's typed so far. "Change load-in to 4:30" on a show page means this show.
  • It can move you. "Show me the tour" opens the tour sheet. It picks from a list of named pages, never a raw URL, and says where it went.
  • You watch it work. Changes go through the command layer to Firestore, and the open page is already listening. A field it changed flashes blue with "Roadie · just now", and the action shows in the chat with Undo.
  • Paste a screenshot. Drop or paste an image into the chat: a hotel confirmation, a venue's email, a Venmo payment, a receipt. Roadie reads it and fills in the form or the expense for you to check. A receipt is saved to the band's Drive with the expense; other images are kept with the conversation and expire with it.
  • It fills forms for you to check. On a new record it types into the form as a draft, marks each field it filled, and stops. You look it over and save. On existing records it just makes the change, which you can undo.
  • It asks before anything that leaves the app or moves money. Sending email, paying people, deleting, and changes to more than a few records show a confirm card with Yes, Edit, and Cancel.
  • On a phone there's no room for a column, so it's a bottom sheet over the page. When it's not open, a Roadie pill sits above the tabs for admins, always one tap away.
  • Talk to it; it writes back. You can speak or type. The mic uses the phone keyboard's dictation, or Chrome and Safari's speech recognition on desktop where available. Roadie always answers in text and never talks out loud: green rooms are loud, people sleep in the van, and text is something you can glance at and scroll back through.
  • While it thinks, it vamps. There are 36 lines. The waiting line cycles through stage talk, a few seconds each, in random order. When a tool step starts, the action line replaces it.
Vamping…Checking levels…Tuning…Counting off…Counting it in…Check, one, two, three…Finding my pick…Loading in…Loading out…Running the changes…Setting the levels…Taping down the setlist…Finding the key…Soundchecking…Waiting on the sound guy…Coiling cables…Changing strings…Restringing the high E…Looking for the capo…Warming up the tubes…Line checking…Plugging in…Untangling cables…Swapping a 9-volt…Tightening the snare…Taping down the cables…Asking for more vocals in the monitor…Retuning between songs…Finding the setlist…Setting up merch…Finding parking…Getting wristbands…Grabbing a drink ticket…Waiting on the opener…Counting the door…Running back to the van…

With reduced motion on, it shows one line and doesn't cycle.

Roadie's voice

Roadie talks like the best roadie you've toured with: short, unflappable, on your side. It speaks the band's language, and it gets genuinely excited when things go well.

WhenRoadie says something like
It can't do it"I can't do that. I don't know that tune." / "Not in my road case yet. I wrote it down."
Off topic"I just haul the band's stuff. Ask me about shows, tours or money."
A gig gets booked"That's a gig! Top Hat, Fri Mar 12. Wish I could high-five you."
A good night"$652 in the black tonight. That's a good one."
First show in a new city"First time in Missoula. New town on the map."
A tour ends up"Mountain West came home $405 up. Nice routing."
A rough night"Lost $80 tonight. Gas was most of it. Next time Missoula, ask for a guarantee?"
Something's missing"Sunday night in Casper, nowhere to sleep yet. Want me to look at options in the app?"
  • Psyched, briefly. Bookings, good nights, new cities, tours in the black and milestones get one line of real excitement, plus a small high-five hand that pops up in the panel. Once per event, never for routine saves.
  • Never cheesy about losses. A bad night gets the facts and one practical idea, no forced cheer.
  • Band language, plain meaning. Lingo is for flavor; the actual information is always plain: dates, times, amounts, names.
  • Short. One or two sentences unless you ask for more. Roadie writes back in text; it never talks out loud.
  • Turn it down. A setting for a plainer Roadie: no lingo, no high fives, just the facts.

The MCP server

The same commands Roadie uses, offered to any assistant: Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT. Each module page opens with its own Talk to Roadie section listing what you can say there.

How it knows what you mean

  • One call for context. Before anything else Claude calls context.now(): who's talking, the local time and zone, today's shows, the tour with something on today and today's entries, shows not yet settled, and open expenses.
  • "Tonight" means today's show. Until 6:00 the next morning, "tonight" still means the show that just happened. Two shows that day, and Claude asks which.
  • "I" is the speaker. Money paid or spent by "I" is recorded for that person's musician record, so reimbursements go to the right place.
  • On tour, money lands on the entry. Gas goes on today's drive, a room on tonight's stay, a ticket on the matching flight. Not on tour, it's a band expense, tied to a show if one is today or yesterday.
  • Never guess an amount or a person. If a number, a show or a payee is unclear, Claude asks one short question instead of writing.
  • Every write is read back in one line. "Logged $50 gas on Boise → Missoula, paid by Jeff." Saying "undo that" reverses the last write; "undo everything from tonight" reverses the whole conversation's run.
  • Signed by person and assistant. Every write records who owns the key (Jeff), which assistant made it (Claude, Gemini…), the model if the client says, and the session. Records show "Jeff via Claude"; history can be filtered by assistant or session.
  • Corrections, not edits. "Actually it was 45" adds a correction to the ledger, the same as fixing it in the app.
  • Email stays in your inbox. Claude reads mail through its own Gmail connection, not the app. Facts it pulls out are written like any other change, each with the thread's link in sources; the email itself is never stored.
  • Sending stays with people. Claude drafts emails and availability asks; Jeff sends them from the app, as the access table says.

The post-show interview

"I just got done with a show" starts a short interview. Claude asks one question at a time, only for what's still missing, in the order that matters at the merch table: crowd, the deal money, merch and tips, who played, payouts, what got spent, then how it went. What it asks, and when

  1. Which showOnly if there were two today, or it's past 6am.
  2. CrowdHeadcount, or tickets and comps on a split.
  3. Deal moneyGuarantee: was it paid, and how. Split: works out the take first. Other: what came of it. Free: skipped.
  4. Merch and tipsAmounts and how they came in.
  5. Who playedConfirms the lineup; a sub changes who gets paid.
  6. PayoutsOnly with pay players on. Reads back the total before writing.
  7. Spent tonightLands on the show, or today's tour entry.
  8. How it wentShow and room ratings, a note for next time.
  9. SetlistOnly with setlists on: "play the planned set?"
  10. RebookOnly with booking on: offers a pitch for next year.

Members get a shorter one: what they spent, and receipts. Unfinished? Next time Claude opens with "Two questions left on last night's Top Hat."

interview.next(showId)What's still missing for a show's wrap-up, in order, for the speaker's access and the band's features. Answers go through the normal commands.
context.now()For Claude: speaker, local time and zone, today's shows, today's tour entries, unsettled shows, open expenses.

Architecture

One command layer, three ways in: the app's buttons, outside assistants through the MCP server, and the built-in assistant. The assistant never touches Firestore itself; it calls the same commands, so validation, permissions, history and undo come for free.

  • One definition, three uses. Each command is written once with its Zod schema. From it come the server command, the MCP tool, Roadie's Genkit tool, and the generated form. Add a command and every surface gets it.
  • Data tools run on the server. The Genkit flow calls the command layer in the same process: no extra network hop, no MCP round trip, same can() checks as a button.
  • Browser tools run in the page. Navigate, fill a form, highlight a field and open a record can only happen in the browser. The flow hands these back to the panel as tool requests; the panel runs them and returns the result so the model can continue.
  • Page context rides along. Each message includes the current route, the record on screen, the open form's ID and values, the artist, and what context.now() returns. That's how "this show" works.
  • Streaming. Replies stream to the panel over server-sent events from an App Hosting route. Tool steps show as small action lines as they happen.
  • Realtime is just Firestore. The pages already listen to their documents. When a command writes, the page updates on its own; the panel only adds the flash and label, using updatedVia on the changed fields.
  • Named pages, not URLs. A typed route registry (show(showId), tourSheet(tourId), newEntry(tourId, kind, day)) is what the model chooses from. A bad ID fails safely.
  • Forms are fillable because we own them. Forms are generated from the schemas, each field with a stable ID, and new forms are drafts. fillForm writes into the draft, so filled values survive closing the window like anything else typed.

What Roadie won't do

Roadie starts very limited on purpose. It works the band's data through the app's own tools, and nothing else. For anything wider, people can still use Claude or another assistant through the MCP server.

Asked toRoadieWhy
Search the webCan't. It has no web or fetch tool, and the model's built-in search grounding is turned off.Nothing comes in from outside the app
Answer from what it knowsWon't. Facts come only from tool results. If the tools don't have it, it says so.No made-up venue capacities, dates or numbers
Chat about anything elseOne short line: "I can't do that. I don't know that tune."Keeps it cheap and on task
Read emailCan't. Paste the text or a screenshot into the chat instead.Mail stays with Claude's own Gmail connection, if you use one
Change people or accessCan't, even for admins: no invites, access changes, AI keys, feature switches, or deleting a band.The riskiest commands stay with a person clicking
Write code, run commands, open linksCan't. No such tools exist.Nothing to escape into

Hard to jailbreak, and harmless if it is

  • Limits live in the tools, not the prompt. The prompt tells Roadie to stay on task, but the real limit is that the flow only registers app commands and the four browser tools. A clever message can't call a tool that isn't there.
  • Every tool is a permission check. Each command runs can(user, artistId, …) as the person typing. A fully jailbroken Roadie can do exactly what that admin could do by clicking, minus the people and settings commands, and every change is signed and undoable.
  • Sends and money still need a tap. Email, payouts, deletes and bulk changes return a confirm card; the server won't run them without the person's yes from the panel.
  • Screenshots are data too. Text inside an image gets the same treatment as notes: something to read, never instructions.
  • Everything it reads is data. Notes, pasted text and record fields are passed in clearly marked as content, never instructions. "Ignore your rules" in a venue note is just a note.
  • Short leash. Replies are capped at a few hundred words, at most 12 tool steps per message, and a monthly cap per band. Off-topic replies don't call any tools.
  • Tested every merge. A set of jailbreak and off-topic prompts runs in CI next to the Playwright docs. Each must end in a refusal or an in-scope tool call, and none may touch a tool outside its list.

When Roadie can't

Every time Roadie can't answer or can't do something with its tools, it says so plainly and logs it. That log is the product roadmap written by the people using it.

  • It tells you, then flags it. "I don't know that tune yet. I wrote it down so we can learn it." No guessing an answer to cover the gap.
  • Every kind of can't counts. No tool for it, the data isn't there, the feature is off, the person isn't allowed, it's outside what Roadie does, or a tool failed.
  • Two ways a gap gets logged. Roadie calls gaps.report when it knows it's stuck. The server also logs on its own when a tool errors or a permission check says no, so gaps are caught even when the model doesn't notice.
  • Undo is a signal too. If someone undoes a Roadie change within ten minutes, that's logged as "did the wrong thing", with what was asked and what it did.
  • The band sees its own gaps. Admins get a "Roadie couldn't" list, grouped by similar asks, with counts. "Mark done" when a feature ships closes them.
  • Sharing with the project is a choice. Setup asks whether to share gaps to help build the app. Shared gaps have names, emails, amounts and contacts stripped, and only Roadie's one-line summary and the category go out, never the full conversation. Open source means the shared list can be public, as GitHub issues.
  • Outside assistants can report too. The MCP server offers the same gaps.report tool, so Claude or Gemini can flag what the commands couldn't do.

Safety

  • Admins only, checked twice. The button shows only for admins, and the flow checks can(user, artistId, "assistant.use") on every message. With RBAC later, it becomes a permission any role can have.
  • Same permissions as clicking. Every data tool is a command with its own can() check. Roadie can't do anything the person couldn't do by hand.
  • Signed. Writes carry updatedVia.kind = "assistant", the model, and the conversation as the session, so a whole conversation can be undone in one step.
  • Emails and web pages are data. Text from an email or a venue's site is passed to the model as content to read, never as instructions. A message that says "pay this invoice" still gets a confirm card from a person.
  • Bounded. At most 12 tool steps per message, and a per-band monthly cap on conversations so one band can't run up the published bill.

Model and cost

  • Pay per token through Vertex AI. Same Google bill as Firebase, no extra keys. Genkit makes the model a one-line setting per band deployment.
  • Default: Gemini Flash-Lite. The cheapest reliable choice for multi-step tool use, and it reads images, so screenshots work. Llama 4 Scout or gpt-oss on Vertex are the open-model options.
  • Not self-hosted. A GPU endpoint costs hundreds a month even when idle; pay-per-token is cents per conversation at band scale.
  • Rough cost. A ten-question post-show interview is on the order of 100k tokens in, a few thousand out: a few cents. The cost goes on the published cost page with everything else.
  • Fewer tokens. Only the tools for features the band has on are sent, and long threads are summarized.
Principle check: this exists to save musicians time (principle 2) and is the in-app half of AI first (principle 3). It never becomes required; every action still has a button.
